Guide • Art Selection September 2026

How to Choose the Perfect Reference Photo for Custom Charcoal Portraits

Commissioning a handcrafted portrait is one of the most personal ways to honor a loved one, celebrate a milestone, or preserve a cherished memory. However, the realism and emotional weight of the finished artwork rely heavily on the quality of the reference photograph provided to the artist.

1. Lighting & Contrast Are Key

High-contrast photos with clear light sources produce dramatic shadows and highlights. Charcoal and graphite artists rely on tonal depth—the range between absolute jet black and crisp paper white—to build three-dimensional volume on flat paper. Avoid overly flat, washed-out images taken with heavy camera flash.

2. Zoom & Facial Details

When drawing eyes, hair texture, and fine expressions, sharp detail is essential. Ensure your image retains clarity when zoomed in on a smartphone screen. If key features like the iris or facial expression are blurry, capturing exact likenesses becomes significantly harder.

3. Emotional Authenticity Over Perfection

Studio poses are wonderful, but candid photos often carry the deepest emotions. A genuine laugh, a natural tilt of the head, or a characteristic smile makes for a striking, soulful portrait that feels alive when rendered in graphite.

Mediums • Technical Insights September 2026

Charcoal vs. Graphite: Which Medium Fits Your Artwork Vision?

At Bantse's Pencil (TheHandOfXpressions), clients frequently ask about the subtle differences between compressed charcoal and technical graphite pencil. Both mediums create stunning monochromatic artwork, but each delivers a distinct visual feeling and texture.

Graphite Realism

  • Produces subtle metallic sheen and clean micro-details.
  • Ideal for intricate skin pores, soft baby portraits, and crisp metallic textures.
  • Offers a wide range of hardness grades (9H to 9B) for ultra-fine control.

Willow & Powdered Charcoal

  • Delivers intense, matte, velvet blacks without light glare.
  • Perfect for high-contrast memorial portraits and bold dramatic artwork.
  • Allows deep blending techniques for smooth, rich background gradients.

In most of our custom size commissions (A4 up to A1 frames), we combine high-grade graphite pencils with dark charcoal powders on heavy acid-free paper to achieve both razor-sharp detail and deep atmospheric depth.

Preservation • Framing Guide September 2026

How to Frame and Protect Original Graphite Artwork for Decades

An original hand-drawn portrait is an investment intended to last generations. Because raw charcoal and graphite particles sit directly on paper fibers, proper protection and mounting are crucial to prevent smudging, yellowing, or environmental humidity damage.

1. Professional Fixative Spray Application

Every drawing finished in our studio is treated with a non-yellowing, archival-grade spray fixative. This coats the loose carbon dust without altering tonal intensity or paper texture.

2. Always Use a Glass Frame with Mount Board

Never press artwork directly against frame glass. Moisture can collect on the inside of the glass and transfer onto the paper. Using a passe-partout mat board creates a 2mm air gap that keeps the drawing pristine.

3. Direct Sunlight Avoidance

Even though graphite is highly lightfast, natural paper can yellow over decades under harsh, direct UV exposure. Hang your framed portrait in a well-lit interior wall away from harsh direct sunlight.
